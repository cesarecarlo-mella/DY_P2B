\section{Notation}

\paragraph{$V$-vertex labels} (the coupling is stripped off and written in the ``coupling'' column):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
label & $V$ attached to & coupling \\
\midrule
$v$, $a$ & open quark line 1 (flavour $q$) & $v_q$, $a_q$ \\
$v_2$, $a_2$ & open quark line 2 (flavour $Q$, four-quark channels) & $v_Q$, $a_Q$ \\
$v\PS$, $a\PS$ & closed quark loop = \textbf{pure singlet (PS)}, summed over $n_f$ flavours & $\sum_fv_f$, $\sum_fa_f$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The couplings are real numbers (tree-level electroweak couplings; the $Z$ propagator, complex in the
Breit--Wigner form, multiplies the whole hadronic tensor and is not part of them), so they factor out of every
$2\Re$ below. Lines are labelled \emph{by line}, not by flavour (identical quarks: see \texttt{Larin.pdf}). PS exists only in loop
amplitudes: RRR, RR, R, LO have none.

\paragraph{Pieces.} $Y^{(j)}X^{(i)*}$ = amplitude with label $Y$ at $j$ loops times the complex conjugate of the
amplitude with label $X$ at $i$ loops, summed over spins, colours, polarisations, couplings stripped off, contracted
with the symmetric leptonic projector $L_1$ (parity-even pieces, table~\ref{tab:even}) or with the antisymmetric
projector $L_6$ (parity-odd pieces, one vector and one axial label, table~\ref{tab:odd}; leptonic coupling
$2v_\ell a_\ell$ instead of $v_\ell^2+a_\ell^2$).

\paragraph{$\gamma_5$.} Same-line parity-even products: anticommuting $\gamma_5$, $aa=vv$: these rows are $v\,v^*$
times $v_q^2+a_q^2$. Rows marked \textbf{(L)}, and all parity-odd rows: Larin for \emph{every} axial vertex of the
piece and of its counterterm pieces.

\paragraph{Conventions.} $a=\alpha_s(\mu^2)/(2\pi)$, $d=4-2\ep$, $\mu=\mu_0$; the bare $L$-loop amplitude (in terms of
$\bar\alpha_s$) is multiplied by $S_\ep^{-L}$, $S_\ep=(4\pi)^\ep e^{-\ep\gamma_E}$ (arXiv:2306.10170, eq.~(5.1)).
\[
\beta_0=\tfrac{11}{6}C_A-\tfrac23T_Rn_f,\qquad \beta_1=\tfrac{17}{6}C_A^2-\tfrac53C_AT_Rn_f-C_FT_Rn_f .
\]

\section{Why the ``\texorpdfstring{$=0$}{=0}'' entries vanish}\label{sec:zero}

All of them are PS pieces: $V$ on a closed quark loop, with the loop attached to the rest of the diagram by gluons.

\paragraph{Furry's theorem (charge conjugation).} For every loop orientation there is the diagram with the
opposite orientation. With $C\gamma^\mu C^{-1}=-(\gamma^\mu)^T$ and $C\gamma^\mu\gamma_5C^{-1}=+(\gamma^\mu\gamma_5)^T$, the reversed
loop equals the original one times $(-1)^{n_v}$, $n_v$ = number of \emph{vector} vertices on the loop (gluons and
$V$ if vector), with the colour trace read in the opposite order. For $V$ plus two gluons the colour trace is
$\tr(T^aT^b)=T_R\delta^{ab}$, the same in both orders, so
\begin{itemize}
\item vector $V$: $n_v=3$, the two orientations cancel: $v\PS=0$;
\item axial $V$: $n_v=2$, they add: $a\PS\neq0$ (the anomalous triangle).
\end{itemize}
Two gluons on the loop is the case of: (i) $V$ + one parton at one loop ($q\bar q\to Vg$, $qg\to Vq$: one gluon to
the quark line, one external); (ii) $q\bar q\to V$ at two loops (both gluons to the quark line); (iii) four-quark
channels at one loop (one gluon to each quark line). Hence the zeros:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
entry & vanishing amplitude & case \\
\midrule
RV $v_q\sum v_f$; RV $a_q\sum v_f$ (odd) & $v\PS^{(1)}$, $m=1$ & (i) \\
RVV 1L$\times$1L $v_q\sum v_f$, $a_q\sum v_f$, $\sum v_f\sum a_f$, $(\sum v_f)^2$ & $v\PS^{(1)}$, $m=1$ & (i) \\
VV $v_q\sum v_f$; VV $a_q\sum v_f$ (odd) & $v\PS^{(2)}$, $m=0$ & (ii) \\
RRV four-quark $v_\ell\sum v_f$, $a_\ell\sum v_f$ & $v\PS^{(1)}$, four-quark & (iii) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With \emph{three} gluons on the loop the vector part survives: $n_v=4$, and the two orders of the trace add,
$\tr(T^aT^bT^c)+\tr(T^cT^bT^a)=\tfrac12d^{abc}$, while for the axial part they subtract, $\propto if^{abc}$. That is why
$v\PS^{(2)}$ for $m=1$, $v\PS^{(1)}$ for $q\bar q\to Vgg$, $qg\to Vqg$, $gg\to Vq\bar q$, and $gg\to Vg$ are non-zero.

\paragraph{Colour.} $gg\to Vg$ at one loop: vector $\propto d^{abc}$, axial $\propto f^{abc}$ (previous paragraph), so the
parity-odd interference $v\PS^{(1)}a\PS^{(1)*}\propto d^{abc}f^{abc}=0$ (symmetric times antisymmetric).

\note{\textbf{Reading the tables.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Counterterms are lower-loop pieces \emph{of the same labels}. So some Larin lower-order pieces are needed even
though the lower order itself uses $aa=vv$: RRV $a_qa_Q$ needs RR $\pc a0{a_2}0$ (L); RVV $a_q\sum a_f$ needs RV
$\pc{a\PS}1a0$ and R $\pc a0a0$ (L), and VV $a_q\sum a_f$ needs B $\pc a0a0$ (L) (the $1/\ep$ of the PS counterterm
times Larin's $aa\neq vv$ at $O(\ep)$ gives a finite difference).
\item $-2C_F$ and the $a^2$ term above are Larin's non-singlet renormalisation $Z_a^{ns}$ of the axial vertex;
$C_FT_R(\frac3{2\ep}+\frac34)$ is the PS constant $Z_s$ (per flavour). The two-loop $Z_a^{ns}$ appears only in the
parity-odd table: the parity-even same-line products use $aa=vv$.
\item With $n_f=5$ massless flavours $\sum_fa_f\neq0$ (the $b$ has no partner): the $\sum a_f$ rows are the ``$b$
remainder'' and need a decision on the top (\texttt{Larin.pdf}, sect.~5).
\item In the parity-odd table each coupling has two orderings (vector in the amplitude, axial in the conjugate, and
the reverse); the counterterm of each ordering carries the $Z$ of the amplitude label at two loops, of the conjugate
label in 1L$\times$1L.
\item Identical quarks ($q=Q$): same rows with $v_Q=v_q$, $a_Q=a_q$; pieces kept separate by line.
\end{itemize}}

\paragraph{Origin of the coefficients.} The amplitude with $m$ emissions is
$\bar a^{\,m/2}\big(M^{(0)}+\bar aM^{(1)}+\bar a^2M^{(2)}\big)$ with
$\bar a=a\big[1-\frac{\beta_0}{\ep}a+\big(\frac{\beta_0^2}{\ep^2}-\frac{\beta_1}{2\ep}\big)a^2\big]$, times
$Z_a^{ns}$ for each Larin axial vertex, plus $Z_s$ times the tree with $V$ on the open lines for $a\PS$. Expanding
in $a$ and keeping the stated order gives every row; the 1L$\times$1L row is the square of the renormalised one-loop
amplitude, the 2L$\times$0L row the renormalised two-loop amplitude times the tree. Check: for the vector, the two RVV
rows together give $-\frac{2\beta_0}{\ep}\,2\Re[\pc v1v0]+\big(\frac{\beta_0^2}{\ep^2}-\frac{\beta_1}{2\ep}\big)\pc v0v0$,
which is the expansion of $\bar a^{\,1}$ and $\bar a^{\,2}$ in the bare $|M|^2$; the parity-odd $v_qa_q$ rows
together give in addition one power of $Z_a^{ns}$. The PS rows reproduce arXiv:2306.10170 eq.~(5.14), the Larin
axial one eq.~(5.13) (with $2C_F\beta_0/\ep$; the paper prints $2\beta_0/\ep$).
